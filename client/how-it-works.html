Section 1 - The Process

Four steps. No shortcuts.

Every builder goes through the same process. No special tracks, no shortcuts. Structure is the point.

01 - Browse open problems
Browse open problems

Every problem on SproutNet is real. Posted by NGOs, companies, government bodies, or individuals with genuine challenges they need solved.

Each problem includes a full brief: context, constraints, what a good solution looks like, and what domain it falls under. Read it carefully — the detail matters.

02 - Submit your solution
Submit your solution

You draft and submit your solution using our 7-field framework — like professionals do on real projects.

The framework tests your problem understanding through to root cause analysis, implementation depth, and risk assessment. Save your draft anytime, and submit when ready for evaluation.

03 - Get evaluated fairly
Get evaluated fairly

All submissions are judged blind. The evaluators don't see your name, your college, or your year. They see only your thinking.

Scoring is done on five criteria: Clarity, Feasibility, Root Cause Analysis, Impact Potential, and Implementation Depth. Each is rated 1 to 10. No favouritism. No connections. Only your work.

04 - Build your Builder Score
Build your Builder Score

Your Builder Score is a cumulative measure of your thinking across all problems. It grows with every strong submission, every milestone completed, every problem you take seriously.

It lives on your public profile. It shows up on the leaderboard. It is the only credential that matters here.

"Most hackathon problems are invented for the event. Every SproutNet problem belongs to someone who actually needs it solved."
Section 2 - The 7-Field Framework

The 7-Field Submission Framework.

Every submission — no exceptions — must complete all seven fields. This isn't bureaucracy. It's the structure that separates a real solution from a vague idea.

Field 1
Problem Understanding

Restate the problem in your own words. What is actually happening? Who is affected? What does the current situation look like, and why is it a problem worth solving?

Field 2
Root Cause Analysis

Go one level deeper. What is causing the problem — not just the symptoms? Use frameworks if helpful: 5 Whys, fishbone, systems thinking. Don't stop at the surface.

Field 3
Proposed Solution

What do you propose? Be specific. Not "an app" — what kind, for whom, doing what exactly? Describe the mechanism, not just the idea.

Field 4
Feasibility Assessment

Can this actually be built? With what resources, by whom, in what timeframe? What are the technical, financial, and organisational requirements? Be honest about constraints.

Field 5
Expected Impact

If this works, what changes? Quantify where possible. Who benefits, by how much, over what period? How does success get measured?

Field 6
Risks & Limitations

What could go wrong? What assumptions are you making? What would cause this solution to fail? Strong solutions acknowledge their own weaknesses.

Field 7
Implementation Plan

How does this actually get built and deployed? What are the phases? What dependencies exist? This is where abstract proposals become real plans.

"Most students have never been asked to think this completely. Every field is there for a reason. If you find yourself struggling with one — that's the point."
Section 3 - Scoring

Blind judging. Five criteria. No politics.

When your submission goes to evaluation, the judge sees nothing about you. Only your work. Scored on five criteria, each rated 1 to 10.

Criteria 1
Clarity of Thinking

Is the submission easy to follow? Are ideas expressed precisely and without ambiguity? Clarity is not about writing style — it is about the quality of thought.

Criteria 2
Root Cause Depth

Did the submission go beyond symptoms to identify the actual cause of the problem? Shallow analysis scores poorly here regardless of how well-written it is.

Criteria 3
Solution Feasibility

Is the proposed solution realistic? Does it account for actual constraints — technical, financial, operational? Ambitious is fine. Impossible is not.

Criteria 4
Impact Potential

If implemented, how meaningful is the outcome? Is it clearly articulated? Are the assumptions reasonable?

Criteria 5
Implementation Depth

Does the submission go beyond the idea to explain how it would actually be executed? Plans beat proposals every time.

How scores become Builder Score
Your average score across all five criteria is multiplied by 10 and adjusted for solutions completed. The formula rewards both quality and consistency — a string of good submissions matters more than one exceptional one.
"A single outstanding submission will not outrank someone who thinks carefully across five problems. Consistency is the skill we are measuring."

Section 6 - FAQ

FAQ

Do I need to know how to code?

No. SproutNet is about structured thinking, not technical implementation. Problems span healthcare, agriculture, civic technology, education, and more. The framework is discipline-agnostic.

Can I work in a team?

Phase 1 submissions are individual. Team submissions are on the roadmap for Phase 2. For now, the thinking is yours alone.

How long do I have to submit?

Each problem has a submission deadline. You can save your draft as you work and submit anytime before the deadline shown on the problem page.

What happens after I submit?

Your submission enters the judging queue. Blind review is completed within 14 days of the problem deadline. Scores and feedback are published to your dashboard when judging is complete.

Can I see other submissions?

Submissions are private during the active judging period. After judging closes, top-ranked submissions may be published as impact stories with the builder's permission.

Is this free?

Yes. SproutNet is completely free for students. Always.
